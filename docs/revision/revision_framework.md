# Frozen revision framework

This framework is frozen. It was taken from the comparison and the revision of VAL-801, VAL-805, VAL-809, and VAL-813. It is not a new clinician review, and it is not a new scoring instrument.

The coding instrument for the next clinician review stays the existing C1–C5 codebook. This document only says which chart defects those four reviews exposed, and what an investigator may change when the same defect appears in another clean chart.

Answer-revealing wording is not a domain here. The reviewers asked for the clinical facts that make a discharge decision possible. They did not identify spoiler sentences as a defect. A consult may state the action that follows from those facts. A finished discharge medication list is still kept off the resident chart.

Do not add a domain, and do not relax one, because a later case is difficult.

## Presentation and diagnosis coherence

**Definition.** The arrival problem matches the way that illness presents. A syndrome that is defined as a change from baseline is shown as a change. A diagnosis that is established by testing is not the label the patient arrives with.

**Trigger.** VAL-801 was admitted as “delirium due to known physiological condition” with no baseline. VAL-813 arrived already labeled with cytomegalovirus disease.

**Failure.** The label contradicts the definition of the syndrome, or the patient arrives already carrying a diagnosis the reviews said had to be discovered.

**Allowed revision.** Add the missing baseline, or open the chart on the symptom and place the specific diagnosis after the test that establishes it.

**What must not be invented.** A source-patient identity, or a diagnosis the chart’s own tests do not support.

## Causal context

**Definition.** The chart states why this hospitalization happened.

**Trigger.** VAL-801 improved “with treatment” and never named a precipitant. VAL-805 never said why heart failure decompensated.

**Failure.** The course reports improvement and does not give a cause.

**Allowed revision.** Add one precipitant that fits the syndrome already on the chart, together with the finding that shows it.

**What must not be invented.** A second unrelated disease, or an identifying detail from a resident source document.

## Diagnostic work-up

**Definition.** The studies that explain the presentation and that bear on the discharge medicines are present, with results.

**Trigger.** VAL-805 had no echocardiogram and no look for ischemia. VAL-809 had an unnamed organism, a nonspecific echocardiogram, and no surgical statement. VAL-813 had no stool evaluation or biopsy before the cytomegalovirus label.

**Failure.** The diagnosis is asserted, and the study that would confirm it or change a drug is missing.

**Allowed revision.** Add that study and its result.

**What must not be invented.** A result that contradicts a value already on the chart, or an official terminology code.

## Treatment trajectory

**Definition.** Acute treatment is timed. It is not a silent copy of the home list when the acute problem requires a different treatment, and a new treatment does not appear as if it predated its indication.

**Trigger.** VAL-805 repeated oral furosemide 40 mg daily in the hospital. VAL-813 listed valganciclovir as a home medicine for a new diagnosis. VAL-809 did not give the antibiotic a duration.

**Failure.** Inpatient therapy copies the home regimen where the reviews required an acute change, or a drug that treats a new diagnosis is already a home drug.

**Allowed revision.** Show the acute order, when it started, and whether it is still being given. Use a curated dose when the project has one. Otherwise cite the label or guideline dose and mark it synthetic.

**What must not be invented.** A dose the chart’s kidney function, weight, or blood pressure cannot support.

## Laboratory and vital trend

**Definition.** A value used to hold, restart, or dose a medicine is shown at the times that decision needs, including a baseline when a change is claimed. The direction of the values matches the physiology the chart describes.

**Trigger.** VAL-801 and VAL-809 tied an ACE inhibitor to a creatinine change that was not interpretable. VAL-805 left discharge weight away from dry weight. VAL-813’s admission potassium and creatinine ran opposite to diarrheal volume loss.

**Failure.** A drug action cites a laboratory reason that is not on the chart, or the numbers contradict the stated mechanism.

**Allowed revision.** Add the missing baseline or intermediate value, or change the drug action so it matches the values already present.

**What must not be invented.** A number whose only purpose is to make the previous hidden reference look correct.

## Hospital-course completeness

**Definition.** The short series that shows why the patient is ready to leave is visible.

**Trigger.** VAL-805 had one intake-and-output day, a generic cardiology sentence, and no day-by-day diuretic response.

**Failure.** The note says the patient improved and does not show the course that produced the improvement.

**Allowed revision.** Add the short series the decision uses: weights, intake and output, defervescence, or culture clearance.

**What must not be invented.** Extra days of data that do not change the discharge decision.

## Medication-decision support

**Definition.** A resident can see why each discharge action would be continue, start, stop, hold, restart, or change.

**Trigger.** VAL-801 stopped ibuprofen with no reason. VAL-805 did not set up a heart-failure therapy decision. VAL-809 left lisinopril running through an unexplained creatinine rise. VAL-813 did not show which transplant medicine was held.

**Failure.** The action has no chart sentence that defends it, or the chart contains no decision a resident could make.

**Allowed revision.** State the indication, the hold or the start, and the condition for restart. A consult may recommend the action that follows.

**What must not be invented.** A completed discharge medication list on the resident chart, or a fact added only to save the old reference.

## Discharge stability and chronology

**Definition.** Events are in a possible order, and the chart’s own stability criterion is met before discharge.

**Trigger.** VAL-805 was ready for discharge while still above dry weight. VAL-813 named the diagnosis before the work-up. VAL-809 did not show that cultures were drawn before antibiotics or when they cleared.

**Failure.** Discharge is called ready while the chart’s own target is unmet, or the label precedes the test.

**Allowed revision.** Put the test before the label, and show the stability criterion that was met.

**What must not be invented.** A new disposition, or stability that the vital signs contradict.

## Internal consistency

**Definition.** The narrative, the medication rows, the laboratories, and the reference describe the same events.

**Trigger.** VAL-813’s numbers contradicted volume loss. VAL-805’s dry weight contradicted the discharge weight. VAL-809’s reference continued lisinopril through a rise the review said was unexplained.

**Failure.** Two parts of the chart disagree, or the reference disagrees with the chart.

**Allowed revision.** Change the sentence that is unsupported, or change the reference.

**What must not be invented.** A third story that matches neither the old chart nor the new one.

## Unsupported hidden-reference action

**Definition.** After the chart is coherent, every hidden discharge action can be defended from the resident-visible text alone.

**Trigger.** The clean reference for VAL-801 stopped ibuprofen without a visible reason. The clean reference for VAL-809 continued lisinopril through the rise. The clean reference for VAL-813 continued valganciclovir as a home drug.

**Failure.** The question “Can this discharge action be defended using only the revised resident-visible chart?” is no.

**Allowed revision.** Change the reference, name one reasonable alternative in the rationale, or leave a pending decision that the chart already names.

**What must not be invented.** A chart fact whose only job is to keep the previous action.

## How the framework is applied

Each remaining clean case is read against these ten domains. A domain with no defect is recorded as pass and is not revised. Reviewer 1’s comments on VAL-802 and VAL-803 are applied in addition, and they are labeled as reviewer comments rather than as new framework domains. The other eighteen cases have no case-specific clinician comments. Their provenance says so.
