# G2-003

96-year-old Male with hyponatremia with thiazide use, distinguished from an unknown medicine

## Presentation

The longitudinal record includes a dementia diagnosis. The caregiver's account of baseline was recognition of family and the ability to hold a conversation. Outpatient products in the longitudinal extract: 24 HR metoprolol succinate 100 MG Extended Release Oral Tablet; Hydrochlorothiazide 25 MG Oral Tablet; 24 HR tacrolimus 1 MG Extended Release Oral Tablet [Envarsus]; lisinopril 10 MG Oral Tablet; amLODIPine 2.5 MG Oral Tablet. The patient became unsteady and mildly confused over four days. Baseline function, per the caregiver, was independent walking and full orientation. Hydrochlorothiazide was still being taken. No diarrhea or diuretic overdose was described. Sodium on arrival was 128 mmol/L. The patient was euvolemic on examination and inattentive.

## Hospital course

Repeat sodium, creatinine, and a chest radiograph were obtained before the thiazide was blamed. The radiograph was clear and the patient was not volume overloaded. Hypo-osmolality and hyponatremia were established from the sodium result. The thiazide was the implicated medicine after other causes were not found. Hydrochlorothiazide was stopped on purpose. Isotonic fluid was given. This stop was intentional and was not an incomplete history. Sodium rose from 128 mmol/L to 134 mmol/L. Creatinine was 1.0 mg/dL compared with 1.1 on arrival and 1.0 beforehand. Gait and attention returned to the caregiver's baseline. Hydrochlorothiazide was intentionally discontinued. A separate outpatient medicine was unknown at arrival and was confirmed by the pharmacy rather than stopped. Attention and intake had returned to the caregiver's account of baseline, and the outpatient medication history was no longer incomplete. The discharge medication list itself was still unsigned. Primary care follow-up and a chemistry panel were arranged.
