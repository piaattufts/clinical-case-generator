61-year-old Male with acute confusion with pneumonia found on the chest radiograph

## Presentation

The longitudinal record does not list dementia. Before this illness the patient handled daily activities and recognized family. Outpatient products in the longitudinal extract: 24 HR metoprolol succinate 100 MG Extended Release Oral Tablet; Hydrochlorothiazide 25 MG Oral Tablet; lisinopril 10 MG Oral Tablet; amLODIPine 2.5 MG Oral Tablet. Confusion appeared over one day after three days of cough. The patient had been oriented at a visit the prior week. Purulent cough preceded the confusion. Temperature was 36.9 degrees Celsius, so the diagnosis did not depend on fever. Oxygen saturation was 94 percent. There were focal crackles at the right base and new inattention.

## Hospital course

The chest radiograph showed a right-lower-lobe infiltrate. Blood cultures showed no growth. Chemistry did not show a primary metabolic cause. Pneumonia, and delirium due to that infection, were diagnosed after the radiograph. Neither label was the presenting diagnosis. Azithromycin was given for the infiltrate and the course was completed before discharge planning. Oxygen was weaned to room air. Cough and inattention resolved. Creatinine was 1.2 mg/dL at discharge planning, admission 1.3, prior 1.2. The caregiver agreed this was the usual mental status. The antibiotic course is finished. The outpatient list was confirmed with the pharmacy and is not the same thing as a medicine that was deliberately stopped. Attention and intake had returned to the caregiver's account of baseline, and the outpatient medication history was no longer incomplete. The discharge medication list itself was still unsigned. Primary care follow-up and a chemistry panel were arranged.
