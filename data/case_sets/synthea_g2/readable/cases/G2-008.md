# G2-008

79-year-old Male with new pulmonary edema with heart failure established during the stay

## Presentation

The longitudinal record does not list heart failure. Cardiovascular disease and cardiac medication are present. Outpatient products in the longitudinal extract: 24 HR metoprolol succinate 100 MG Extended Release Oral Tablet; warfarin sodium 5 MG Oral Tablet; lisinopril 10 MG Oral Tablet. No pre-admission creatinine was in the extracted record. The first creatinine used as the pre-treatment baseline was 1.0 mg/dL. The most recent longitudinal weight was 91.4 kg. Over five days dyspnea progressed from exertion to conversation, orthopnea required three pillows, and both legs became swollen. Those symptoms were not the patient's usual state. The patient missed two days of the home antihypertensive medicine. Arrival blood pressure was 186/104, with no focal neurologic deficit and no chest pain. On arrival the weight was 96.9 kg, oxygen saturation was 91 percent on room air, the respiratory rate was 26, the jugular venous pressure was elevated, and crackles were present halfway up both lung fields. There was pitting edema to the knees.

## Hospital course

The chest radiograph showed pulmonary edema. Natriuretic peptide B was elevated. Troponin I remained 0.01 ng/mL and the electrocardiogram did not show infarction. A transthoracic echocardiogram, obtained after the radiograph, showed reduced left-ventricular systolic function and a congested filling pattern. The numeric ejection fraction was estimated at 45 percent. Those findings established Acute systolic (congestive) heart failure. The diagnosis was not used as the presenting label before the examination, radiograph, natriuretic peptide, and echocardiogram. No loop diuretic was in the longitudinal extract. Intravenous furosemide was begun for congestion and then converted to oral furosemide 40 MG daily. Daily weights and net fluid output were recorded. No angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitor or angiotensin-receptor blocker from a curated regimen was held. By hospital day 4 the weight was 92.2 kg, oxygen saturation was 96 percent on room air, the respiratory rate was 16, and the patient could lie flat. Creatinine was 1.0 mg/dL after a peak of 1.1. Potassium was 4.3 mmol/L. Dyspnea had returned to the pre-admission baseline rather than merely being called improved. The patient was walking on the ward, eating, and without resting dyspnea or new chest pain. The unsigned task is the discharge medication plan, not another day of intravenous diuresis. Heart-failure clinic follow-up and a chemistry panel were arranged. Home weight monitoring was requested.
