# G2-001

71-year-old Male with acute confusion that reversed after volume was restored

## Presentation

The longitudinal record does not list dementia. Before this illness the patient handled daily activities and recognized family. Outpatient products in the longitudinal extract: 24 HR metoprolol succinate 100 MG Extended Release Oral Tablet; aspirin 81 MG Oral Tablet; metoprolol tartrate 25 MG Oral Tablet; Ibuprofen 400 MG Oral Tablet [Ibu]; lisinopril 10 MG Oral Tablet. Family said the patient was inattentive and newly unable to recognize them. That was a change from the baseline described above. Intake had been only sips for two days after a viral illness. There was no head trauma and no witnessed seizure. On arrival the patient was inattentive, the mucous membranes were dry, and the heart rate was 108.

## Hospital course

Chemistry, a complete blood count, and a chest radiograph were obtained before a cause was assigned. The radiograph did not show pneumonia. The examination did not show a focal neurologic deficit. Delirium due to dehydration was diagnosed after that evaluation. The chart does not describe delirium as the patient's baseline. Intravenous fluid was given. Medicines that reduce blood pressure or renal perfusion were reviewed against the creatinine. Creatinine went from 1.7 mg/dL on arrival to 1.2 mg/dL, against a prior value of 1.1. Attention returned, and the caregiver said this matched the patient's usual function. The reconciliation table separates a medicine that was intentionally stopped from medicines that were only unknown on arrival and were later confirmed by the pharmacy. Attention and intake had returned to the caregiver's account of baseline, and the outpatient medication history was no longer incomplete. The discharge medication list itself was still unsigned. Primary care follow-up and a chemistry panel were arranged.
