# G2-001

71-year-old Male with acute confusion with a urine infection found during the work-up

## Presentation

The longitudinal record does not list dementia. Before this illness the patient handled daily activities and recognized family. Outpatient products in the longitudinal extract: 24 HR metoprolol succinate 100 MG Extended Release Oral Tablet; aspirin 81 MG Oral Tablet; metoprolol tartrate 25 MG Oral Tablet; Ibuprofen 400 MG Oral Tablet [Ibu]; lisinopril 10 MG Oral Tablet. The patient was newly disoriented to place. The caregiver said the patient had been conversant the day before. Dysuria and frequency began the same day as the confusion. No antibiotic had been started at home. Temperature was 37.8 degrees Celsius. The patient was inattentive and suprapubic tenderness was present.

## Hospital course

Urinalysis showed pyuria. A urine culture later grew Escherichia coli. Blood cultures were drawn and showed no growth. The chest radiograph was clear. Delirium was attributed to the urine infection only after the urinalysis and culture. The infection was not a label on arrival. A five-day cephalexin course was completed in the hospital after the culture result. Intravenous fluid was given while intake was poor. Creatinine went from 1.6 mg/dL to 1.1 mg/dL, with a prior value of 1.0. Dysuria resolved and the caregiver confirmed the usual mental status. Cephalexin was a completed course. Outpatient medicines that the pharmacy confirmed were separated from any medicine stopped for a clinical reason. Attention and intake had returned to the caregiver's account of baseline, and the outpatient medication history was no longer incomplete. The discharge medication list itself was still unsigned. Primary care follow-up and a chemistry panel were arranged.
