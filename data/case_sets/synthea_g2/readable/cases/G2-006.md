# G2-006

61-year-old Female with acute on chronic heart failure

## Presentation

The longitudinal record already lists heart failure. Outpatient products in the longitudinal extract: Ibuprofen 400 MG Oral Tablet [Ibu]. The most recent longitudinal creatinine was 1.1 mg/dL. The most recent longitudinal weight was 77.5 kg. Over five days dyspnea progressed from exertion to conversation, orthopnea required three pillows, and both legs became swollen. Those symptoms were not the patient's usual state. The patient stopped several home cardiovascular medicines ten days ago because the pharmacy refill was unaffordable. Diet and cardiac rhythm were unchanged. On arrival the weight was 83.0 kg, oxygen saturation was 91 percent on room air, the respiratory rate was 26, the jugular venous pressure was elevated, and crackles were present halfway up both lung fields. There was pitting edema to the knees.

## Hospital course

The chest radiograph showed pulmonary edema. Natriuretic peptide B was elevated. Troponin I remained 0.01 ng/mL. There was no focal infiltrate. A transthoracic echocardiogram, obtained after the radiograph, showed reduced left-ventricular systolic function and a congested filling pattern. The numeric ejection fraction was estimated at 30 percent. Those findings established Acute on chronic systolic (congestive) heart failure. The diagnosis was not used as the presenting label before the examination, radiograph, natriuretic peptide, and echocardiogram. No loop diuretic was in the longitudinal extract. Intravenous furosemide was begun for congestion and then converted to oral furosemide 40 MG daily. Daily weights and net fluid output were recorded. No angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitor or angiotensin-receptor blocker from a curated regimen was held. By hospital day 4 the weight was 78.3 kg, oxygen saturation was 96 percent on room air, the respiratory rate was 16, and the patient could lie flat. Creatinine was 1.1 mg/dL after a peak of 1.2. Potassium was 4.3 mmol/L. Dyspnea had returned to the pre-admission baseline rather than merely being called improved. The patient was walking on the ward, eating, and without resting dyspnea or new chest pain. The unsigned task is the discharge medication plan, not another day of intravenous diuresis. Heart-failure clinic follow-up and a chemistry panel were arranged. Home weight monitoring was requested.
